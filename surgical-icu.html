About Surgical ICU (SICU) & Critical Care

The Surgical Intensive Care Unit (SICU) at Dalawai Maternity Hospital serves as our specialized critical care hub. It is specifically engineered to support patients recovering from major gynecological surgeries, high-risk Cesarean sections, and life-threatening obstetric crises such as postpartum hemorrhage (PPH), eclampsia, and severe sepsis.

Operating under rigorous infection control standards, our unit features advanced multi-parameter cardiac monitors, invasive blood pressure lines, mechanical ventilators, infusion pumps, and central medical gas pipelines. Each patient bed is continuously monitored by ICU-trained nurse specialists under the supervision of senior anesthesiologists and critical care physicians.

Because emergency situations require instantaneous decision-making, the SICU is physically positioned immediately adjacent to our major surgical operating complex. This spatial integration allows rapid bedside transfers, emergency bedside procedures, and seamless continuity of clinical care during life-critical moments.

CRITICAL CARE CAPABILITIES

Conditions & Procedures Treated

Advanced intensive support for high-risk surgical and emergency obstetric patients.

01

Severe Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH) Resuscitation

Immediate rapid blood transfusion protocol, invasive hemodynamic monitoring, pharmacologic uterine contraction, and surgical stabilization.

02

Obstetric Eclampsia & Hypertensive Crises

Intensive blood pressure regulation, continuous neuro-vital monitoring, intravenous anti-convulsant therapy, and emergency delivery management.

03

Post-Operative High-Risk Monitoring

Close observation following extensive gynecological cancer operations, total hysterectomies, or high-risk C-sections with underlying medical illness.

04

Mechanical Ventilatory Support

Advanced invasive mechanical ventilation and non-invasive BiPAP/CPAP respiratory support tailored for severe acute lung compromise or distress.

05

Sepsis & Septic Shock Management

Broad-spectrum targeted intravenous antibiotic protocols, central line placement, vasopressor administration, and multi-organ preservation care.

06

Invasive Hemodynamic Monitoring

Continuous arterial line blood pressure tracking, central venous pressure (CVP) monitoring, and precise fluid responsiveness evaluations.

07

Electrolyte & Metabolic Stabilization

Rapid correction of severe electrolyte imbalances, arterial blood gas (ABG) monitoring, diabetic ketoacidosis control, and metabolic balance.

08

Emergency Cardiac & Airway Resuscitation

24/7 rapid response team equipped with automated defibrillators, emergency airway intubation equipment, and advanced cardiac life support (ACLS).

CRITICAL CARE ROADMAP

What to Expect: Patient ICU Journey

Four essential phases from acute emergency admission to step-down ward recovery.

01

Emergency Triage & Admission

Rapid transfer directly from OT or emergency desk into the SICU bed with instant vital sign stabilization.

02

Intensive Stabilization

Continuous multi-para monitoring, arterial blood gas sampling, mechanical ventilation or vasopressor support execution.

03

24/7 Vigilance & Rounds

Round-the-clock intensivist monitoring, frequent family status briefings, and 1:1 dedicated nursing care.

04

Step-Down Ward Transition

Once vitals are completely stable, patient is safely transitioned to private inpatient suite for final recovery.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers regarding ICU policies, visiting hours, and patient monitoring.

To maintain maximum sterility and undisturbed patient rest, visiting hours in the SICU are restricted to designated morning and evening windows (usually 11:00 AM - 12:00 PM and 5:00 PM - 6:00 PM). One immediate family member is permitted entry at a time after sanitization.

Our lead intensivist conducts daily medical counseling sessions with the primary family member, providing clear updates on vital parameters, test results, ventilator status, and treatment plans.

Patients are ready for transfer to an inpatient ward once their vital signs are completely stable, organ support (such as ventilation or vasopressors) is no longer required, and post-operative pain is well controlled.
